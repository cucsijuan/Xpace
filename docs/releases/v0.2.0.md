## What's new

**Radar jammers.** Ships can carry a jammer, run by RWR.COM (U cycles it). Noise fills a radar's receiver: the echo is measured worse, far out it is hidden, and the radar sees a strobe on the jammer's bearing. Deception answers a radar that has the ship locked with copies of its own pulses, walks its gate off and leaves it on nothing, taking a missile on its datalink with it; close in, the real echo is stronger and the trick fails. Models are resources (NJ-1, NJ-2, DJ-3).

**The drive.** Ships fly against the veil, which sets top speeds and turn rates from the hardware: the main engine, the thrusters and the hull. The engine's controller cuts the thrust when its core runs hot; boost cools harder and glows brighter, and the controller has flaws that can be found and used, at a cost.

**Turning.** The veil grips across the hull, and the flight assist's safe turns keep the velocity within a slip window of the nose: tighter, faster turns that come from the ship, not from software limits.

**Player against player.** Multiplayer sessions start with only the players' ships; the scene's drones and AI fighters stay out. In single player the enemies now start behind you, facing away.

**New look for missiles and explosions.** Missiles use the art pack's models, with a flicker at the nozzle, a trail left along the path they fly and puffs of smoke. Warheads and ships blow up in fire, sparks, burning debris and smoke, and a destroyed ship fades into the cloud. The main menu's ship has its paint back and its engines push and let go.

**Docs.** A guide to ships: hulls, thrusters, engines and jammers, field by field, with the formulas and recipes for tiers.
